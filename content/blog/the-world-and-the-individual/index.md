This book marks my first foray into the world of philosophy. It delves into various interpretations of the meaning of the world and the individual, and it is the first volume in the series _The Four Historical Conceptions of Being_ by American philosopher and historian Josiah Royce.

The book is a collection of ten lectures delivered by Royce at the University of Aberdeen in 1899. For me, this is one of the book’s main drawbacks. I found it challenging to connect the ideas, and the topics themselves seemed scattered and repetitive.

In my personal opinion, I did not make the best choice with this book. It is not suitable for beginners in philosophy, and I had to spend a lot of time researching many of the concepts, figures, and philosophical theories mentioned in order to understand their context within the book.

My difficulty was not simply that the ideas were abstract. Royce enters conversations with Plato, Aristotle, Spinoza, Kant, and other philosophers as though the reader has already met them. I had not. Every page opened several other doors, and I spent more time searching for the rooms behind those doors than following Royce through his own argument.

The lecture form intensified the problem. Repetition may have helped an audience hearing the argument over several evenings, but on the page I struggled to see where one conception of being ended and the next began. I kept mistaking my lack of preparation for a judgement about the book.

That recognition was useful. A difficult book is not automatically profound, but neither is confusion proof that the author has failed. Sometimes I have chosen the wrong entrance. _The World and the Individual_ showed me that I needed a map of the philosophical tradition before I could appreciate an argument positioned inside it.

I would not recommend this as a first philosophy book. I also no longer think that makes reading it a mistake. It forced me to learn how much context serious reading can require and left me curious enough to build that context before returning.

## Reference

Royce, J. (1900). [*The World and the Individual: First Series, The Four Historical Conceptions of Being*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/47315). Macmillan.
